Every map we draw as designers — user flows, journey maps, sitemaps, wireframes — rests on one assumption: that we can enumerate the paths in advance.

Generative interfaces break that assumption, and most of our tooling hasn't noticed yet.

## The flow was always a promise about scarcity

A flow diagram says: there are seven things someone might want here, and here is the shortest route to each. It works because the set is small and knowable.

An input box that accepts anything has no such set.

## What replaces it

Not nothing. Three things, so far, in what we've built at Vaatun:

1. **Capability inventories** instead of flows — what the system can actually do, independent of how someone asks for it.
2. **Failure maps** instead of happy paths — the interesting design work is in what happens when the model is wrong, uncertain, or slow.
3. **Trust ladders** — how much a user should be shown before they're asked to act on something a model produced.

## The part I'm still unsure about

Enumerable flows made products testable. You could walk every branch. I don't yet have a good replacement for that, and I'm suspicious of anyone who says they do.
